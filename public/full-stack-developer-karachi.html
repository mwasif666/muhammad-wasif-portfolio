The honest version, before you read further

Plenty of freelancers put "full stack" on a profile because it widens the net. Here is the actual split, so you can decide with real information:

  • Front end: senior. React, Next.js, TypeScript, HTML, CSS, responsive and accessible builds, Core Web Vitals. This is the craft, and it is where four-plus years of daily practice went.
  • CMS and commerce: strong and proven. WordPress and WooCommerce, Shopify theme and Liquid work. Five live stores you can open.
  • Backend: shipping, still growing. Laravel with MySQL and Node with Express and MongoDB are both running in production on delivered projects. I am competent and careful there; I am not going to call it senior.
  • Deployment, APIs and analytics: routine. Build pipelines, hosting, SSL, third-party integrations, Google Tag Manager and Search Console.

What the backend work has actually been

Not theory from a course. Offplan DXB is a Dubai property portal on Laravel and MySQL: listings, filtering and lead capture against a real database. Hope Plants Dubai is a Laravel application with catalogue content managed from an admin side. MindReach pairs a React front end with a Laravel backend.

On the JavaScript side, Inner Beast runs Next.js over a Node, Express and MongoDB stack. Those are the receipts. What they do not yet include is heavy data modelling at scale, or a system where a security mistake is expensive, which is exactly why I flag those rather than take them alone.

When one developer is the right call

Good fit: a business site with a CMS your team edits; a WooCommerce or Shopify store; a marketing platform with forms, integrations and analytics; a React or Next.js front end over an existing backend; an admin panel with roles and reporting on Laravel.

Bring in a specialist: financial or health data, complex multi-tenant permissions, real-time systems at scale, anything where a breach or a wrong number costs real money. In those cases I still do the front end at full strength. I just do not pretend the server side is a solo job.

Every handover between vendors is a place where scope goes missing and nobody owns the bug. Removing that gap is the real value of hiring one person. Pretending the gap does not exist when it does is not.

Frequently asked questions

Are you a full stack developer or a front-end developer?

Senior front-end developer who also ships full stack work. Front end is where the depth is; Laravel with MySQL and Node with MongoDB are in production on delivered projects but I am still building depth there. I would rather you hear that now than discover it mid-project.

So can you build my whole project end to end?

For most business sites, CMS builds, stores and marketing platforms, yes: front end, backend, database, deployment and analytics. For heavy data modelling, real-time systems at scale or security-sensitive work, I will say so at the quote stage and either bring in a backend specialist or take the front end only.

How much does a full stack project cost in Karachi?

It depends entirely on scope. A five-page marketing site and a booking platform with logins, payments and an admin panel are not the same job. Send the pages and features you have in mind and you get a fixed written quote before any work starts.

Which stack will you use?

Whichever suits the problem. Content-led site your team edits: WordPress. Marketing site that must rank and load fast: Next.js. Application with accounts, roles and reporting: React with Laravel. I explain the trade-off before the decision rather than defaulting to one tool.

Can you take over a project another developer started?

Yes, if the code is available and the stack is one I work in. I read what is there, tell you honestly whether it is worth continuing or rebuilding, and give you the reasoning rather than just the verdict.

Tell me what you are building

Send the pages, the features and the deadline. You get an honest answer about scope, including the answer that part of it should go to someone else.
